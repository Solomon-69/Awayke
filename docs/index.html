Awayke

Keep your MacBook awake with the lid closed

Awayke is a small menu bar app. Click the candle and your MacBook stays awake, even with the lid shut. Most keep-awake apps cannot stop lid-close sleep, but Awayke can, and it turns that back off for you.

What it does

Awake when you need it, and no more

Good for overnight builds and downloads, sharing a hotspot from your bag, or keeping a server reachable with the lid shut.

A timer that ends for you

Pick a timer, or add your own from 5 minutes to 30 days. Add 30 minutes while it runs, get a notice 5 minutes before the end, and see the time left in the menu bar if you like. Normal sleep comes back when the timer ends, when you quit or restart, and if the app ever crashes.

Quiet with the lid closed

While Awayke is on and the lid is shut, the screen goes dark, the sound is muted, and the chat apps you choose are paused to save battery. Everything comes back when you open the lid.

Small, native and quiet

No background helper, no kernel extension and no telemetry. It only goes online when you click Check for updates, and an activity log on your Mac shows what it did.

Install

Download, open, then click the candle

Awayke is not sold through an app store, so macOS may ask you to confirm it the first time you open it.

MacYour computer

macOS 26 Tahoe or later, Apple silicon

  1. Download Awayke.dmg and open it.
  2. Drag Awayke into Applications, then open it from there.
  3. A small candle appears in the menu bar. Click it to switch Awayke on.
  4. The first time, Awayke asks for permission once, with Touch ID or your password. Then you can close the lid.

If macOS blocks the first open: right-click Awayke in Applications and choose Open, then confirm. Or go to System Settings, Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

WindowsYour computer

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit

  1. Download Awayke-windows.zip, right-click it and choose Extract All. Keep the folder somewhere permanent, for example Documents.
  2. Open the folder and run Awayke.exe.
  3. If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway.
  4. Click the Awayke icon in the system tray. If you cannot see it, click the small arrow next to the tray icons.

About the one-time permission

Why Awayke asks for your password once

  1. Stopping lid-close sleep needs a protected macOS setting, which normally asks for a password every time.
  2. Awayke asks once and installs one small rule. It lets your account run exactly two commands without a password: turn lid-close sleep off, and back on.
  3. Nothing else. The uninstaller removes the rule.

The full security notes are on GitHub.

Questions

Good to know

Is it safe? Will my Mac overheat or drain the battery?

It is fine for light work you leave running, such as downloads, syncing or a hotspot. Heavy work with the lid fully shut reduces airflow, so use judgement. The timer ends it for you, and you can switch on Turn off at low battery so it stops before the battery runs flat.

I switched it on, closed the lid, and the Mac still slept.

Open the panel and check it says the Mac stays awake. Then, in Terminal, run pmset -g | grep SleepDisabled. It should say 1 while Awayke is on. If it says 0, switch Awayke off and on again to be asked for the permission.

Does it collect any data?

No. Awayke has no analytics and no accounts, and its settings and activity log stay on your Mac. The only time it goes online is when you click Check for updates, which asks GitHub for the latest version number.

Is it open source? Who made it?

Yes, under the MIT licence. Awayke is based on Sleepless by Adam Boudjemaa, and the redesign and changes are by Solomon. The code is on GitHub.

How do I remove it?

Use the uninstall script that comes with the app, in /Applications/Awayke.app/Contents/Resources/uninstall.sh. It restores normal sleep, removes the app and removes the permission rule.

Unsigned app: how to open it

This app is not signed yet (Apple's developer account is paid, and one is not set up yet), so your computer warns you the first time. Mac: right-click the app, choose Open, then Open again (or System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway). Windows: in SmartScreen click More info, then Run anyway. You can check your download against the SHA-256 fingerprints listed in the project's CHECKSUMS.md on GitHub.
